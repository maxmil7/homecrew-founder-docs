# Home Circle — YC Readiness Guide

## What YC Cares About

YC evaluates applications on three things, in roughly this order:

### 1. Founders (Most Important)

You're a technical founder building the product yourself. That's actually a strength — YC respects builders who ship. Marketplaces are hard and they typically like to see at least two founders (one technical, one growth/sales). Solo founders do get accepted — think of Pebble, DoorDash started with one active founder, and plenty of others. Your day job experience as a professional developer also signals competence.

### 2. Traction (What Separates You from 10,000 Other Applicants)

This is where it gets real. YC gets ~30,000 applications per batch and accepts ~200. Having an app with 26 screens and a good idea won't stand out. What will stand out:

- "We launched 8 weeks ago in one neighbourhood in San Jose"
- "We have 200 homeowners and 80 vouched pros"
- "42% of users who sign up vouch for at least one pro within 7 days"
- "Our contact sync rate is 65% and the average user finds 6 friends on the platform"
- "We're growing 15% week over week through organic invites only"

Numbers like these — even small ones — prove the core hypothesis. YC would rather fund an app with 200 passionate users than a polished product with 10,000 passive ones.

### 3. Market (Is This Big Enough?)

Home services is $600B+ in the US. Trust-based discovery is a real gap. Thumbtack, Angi, and HomeAdvisor are lead-gen models that homeowners hate. You have a genuine differentiation. This part of your story is strong.

---

## Realistic Timeline for a YC Application

YC typically has two batches: **Summer** (apply ~March-April, start ~June) and **Winter** (apply ~September-October, start ~January).

If you launch MVP on May 1 and spend May–August building traction, you could apply for the **Winter 2027 batch** (applications likely open September–October 2026). That gives you 5 months of real usage data to put in the application.

## What You'd Need by Application Time

| Metric | Target | Why It Matters |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Active users | 500–1,000 | Shows you can get people to sign up |
| Vouches created | 200+ | Proves core behaviour works |
| Week-over-week growth | 10–15% | Shows organic momentum |
| Retention (30-day) | 30%+ | Users come back |
| One strong neighbourhood | Dominant coverage | Proves the density thesis |

## What Would Make Your Application Compelling

Don't pitch Home Circle as a home maintenance app. Pitch it as:

> *"We're building the trust layer for home services. Homeowners already ask friends for pro recommendations — we're digitizing that behaviour. Our vouch-based model means pros are discovered through real relationships, not ad spend. In our launch neighbourhood, 40% of searches result in a contact through a circle recommendation. We're Nextdoor meets Yelp, but trust-first."*

---

## Honest Risk Factors

**Solo founder** is the biggest flag. If between now and application time you found a co-founder (especially someone with growth/marketing/community building skills), your odds improve significantly. This doesn't mean you need one to apply — but it helps.

**Scaling beyond one neighbourhood** is the second risk. YC will ask "does this scale?" Your answer needs to be a playbook: "Here's how we seeded Willow Glen, here's how we replicate it in Campbell, here's why the model gets easier with each new neighbourhood because existing users have friends in adjacent areas."

---

## Bottom Line

Build the MVP. Launch May 1. Spend the summer obsessively growing one neighbourhood. If the numbers are there by September, apply for Winter 2027. You'll have a real product, real users, and real data — which puts you ahead of 80% of applicants who only have a pitch deck.

And if YC doesn't work out, those same metrics will attract angel investors and pre-seed funds. The traction is what matters, not the accelerator.
